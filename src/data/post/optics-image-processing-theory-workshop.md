This activity was organized by the HDSP research seedbed (semillero 4765) as part of the **Forum on Computational Optics and Deep Learning: Applications and Challenges**, a cycle of four sessions held during the 2026-2 academic semester. The forum was free and open to students from any program at UIS and to anyone interested in research, as part of the seedbed's solidarity outreach project under the 2026 Strengthening Plan of the Vice-Rectorate for Research and Extension (VIE).

The second activity of the forum went back to the fundamentals: what light is and how it behaves. From those principles, the workshop explained how an optical system **forms an image** and what happens when that image reaches a sensor and becomes data.

The workshop then moved on to acquisition: techniques for **capturing information with optical systems** and an introduction to **digital image and signal analysis**. Each concept came with examples from previous laboratory projects, so participants could see the theory working on real research data.

The goal was for participants to leave with the foundations needed to understand how scientific data is acquired and analyzed in an optical system, the starting point for the practical workshop.

## Main Topics

- Fundamental principles of the theory of light
- Basic concepts of image formation
- Capture and data acquisition techniques in optical systems
- Introduction to digital image and signal analysis
- Examples from previous laboratory projects
