During the 2026-2 academic semester, research seedbed 4765 of the HDSP group held the **Forum on Computational Optics and Deep Learning: Applications and Challenges**. The forum ran over **four (4) sessions**, split between the optics laboratory and the auditorium, to show how light is captured, how it becomes data, and what a neural network can do with that data.

The forum was open to students from any program at UIS and to anyone interested in research. It was **free of charge**, as part of the seedbed's solidarity outreach project (Proyecto de Extensión en CTeI Solidario, continuing education modality) under the 2026 Strengthening Plan of the Vice-Rectorate for Research and Extension (VIE).

## Session I: Guided Visit to the Optics Laboratory

The first session took place inside the HDSP optics laboratory. Attendees toured the facilities and saw up close the equipment the group has used in its projects: what each instrument does, how it combines with the others, and which research it has supported.

The tour introduced the laboratory's research lines and some representative projects that bring together optical instrumentation, image processing, and deep learning. With those examples in view, the discussion covered how each setup made it possible to tackle a concrete scientific or technological problem.

The visit closed with a reflection on the role of experimental infrastructure in producing knowledge, and on the opportunities the laboratory offers to anyone who wants to start doing research as an undergraduate.

## Session II: Theoretical Workshop on Optics and Image Processing

The second session went back to the fundamentals: what light is and how it behaves. From those principles, the workshop explained how an optical system forms an image and what happens when that image reaches a sensor and becomes data.

The workshop then moved on to acquisition: techniques for capturing information with optical systems and an introduction to digital image and signal analysis. Each concept came with examples from previous laboratory projects, so participants could see the theory working on real research data.

The goal was for participants to leave with the foundations needed to understand how scientific data is acquired and analyzed in an optical system, the starting point for the practical workshop.

## Session III: Practical Workshop on Optical Systems and Data Acquisition

The third session was hands-on. It began with an introduction to the optical devices available in the laboratory and an explanation of how each experimental component works and what it is used for.

Participants then assembled a basic optical system on their own. With the setup ready, they acquired and processed real data and signals coming from their own experimental system.

This was where the theory from the previous workshop met practice: align, measure, check, and measure again, using the same methodologies applied in a research project.

## Session IV: Keynote Talk. The New Era of Optics: When Light Meets AI

The forum closed with a keynote talk. It started from the theoretical foundations of light and its role in today's science and technology, and moved toward the relationship between optics, image processing, and artificial intelligence.

The talk presented applications across different industries built with deep learning tools, and discussed how combining light and AI is opening new frontiers in scientific and technological research, and why computational optics helps solve complex problems.

The session ended with an open space for questions and discussion between students and researchers, and with an invitation to join the training processes of the seedbed and the research group.
